# Community

The Community collection contains the principles, structures and future practices that support participation around the InfraDev Academy.

It defines how learners, contributors, reviewers, maintainers and partners may interact with the project in a respectful, transparent and sustainable way.

---

## Purpose

This collection exists to support a healthy and learning-oriented project community.

It may provide guidance for:

- participation;
- onboarding;
- communication;
- collaboration;
- contribution;
- support;
- recognition;
- moderation;
- community safety;
- feedback;
- events;
- future partnerships.

The Community collection should help people understand how to participate without confusing social interaction with technical Governance.

---

The collection may contain documents related to:

- community principles;

## Community Principles

The InfraDev Academy Community should be guided by the principles below.

### 1. Respect

Participants should communicate with professionalism, empathy and respect.

### 2. Learning

Questions, explanations and review should support learning rather than humiliation.

### 3. Clarity

Expectations, decisions and participation paths should remain understandable.

### 4. Inclusion

Participation should not depend on status, seniority or prior recognition.

### 5. Safety

The Community should not tolerate harassment, intimidation, exposure of personal data or abusive behavior.

### 6. Responsibility

Participants remain responsible for the quality and impact of their contributions.

### 7. Transparency

Community processes should be visible when they affect participation, moderation or recognition.

### 8. Sustainability

The project should not create community programs it cannot responsibly maintain.

### 9. Knowledge Sharing

Experience should be transformed into reusable knowledge whenever appropriate.

### 10. Engineering Mindset

Discussion should consider evidence, context, trade-offs, risks and maintainability.

## Community and Contribution

Community participation and repository contribution are related but different.

### Community Participation

May include:

- asking questions;
- discussing ideas;
- attending events;
- sharing feedback;
- helping learners;
- reviewing public content;
- suggesting improvements.

### Repository Contribution

May include:

- opening Issues;
- proposing documents;
- updating code;
- submitting Pull Requests;
- reviewing technical content;
- maintaining collections.

The Contributor Guide defines repository contribution.

The Community collection defines broader participation and interaction.

---

## Community and Governance

Community and Governance have different responsibilities.

### Community

Supports participation, communication and relationships.

### Governance

Defines authority, decision-making, approval and change control.

Example:

```text
Community Discussion

Identifies a recurring need.

↓

Governance Process

Evaluates and records the decision.

↓

Implementation

Applies the accepted change.
```

Community opinion may inform decisions.

It does not automatically replace the project's decision process.

## Community and Guides

Guides explain how to perform project activities.

Community documents explain how people participate and interact.

A Community onboarding document may reference:

- Contributor Guide;
- Editorial Handbook;
- repository structure;
- Code of Conduct;
- communication channels;
- current Roadmap.

Avoid copying complete Guide content into Community documents.

## Participation Levels

The project may recognize several forms of participation.

### Reader

Consumes and applies published knowledge.

### Learner

Follows Learning Paths, laboratories and educational resources.

### Participant

Engages in discussions, feedback or community activities.

### Contributor

Creates or improves project artifacts.

### Reviewer

Validates technical, editorial, product or repository quality.

### Maintainer

Supports continuity, architecture, Governance and releases.

### Partner

Supports the project through institutional, educational or technical collaboration.

These levels describe activity.

They should not be used to diminish the value of newer participants.

### Early Stage

- safe feedback;
- manageable communication;
- transparent decisions.

### Growth Stage

The project may introduce:

- regular discussions;
- contributor onboarding;
- mentorship;

- community events;

### Mature Stage

The project may support:

- Schools;
- Programs;
- working groups;
- partner initiatives;
- contributor councils;

Growth should follow demonstrated need, capacity and Governance readiness.

## Current Community State

During the Foundation and early technical releases, Community work should prioritize:

- understandable entry points;
- contributor guidance;
- respectful communication;
- clear repository participation;
- safe handling of real cases;
- transparent feedback;
- realistic expectations.

Large community programs should remain deferred until the project can maintain them consistently.

## Communication Channels

Future Community communication may occur through:

- GitHub Issues;
- GitHub Discussions;
- repository Pull Requests;
- project website;
- scheduled meetings;
- community platforms;
- email;
- future social channels.

Each channel should have a clear purpose.

Example:

```text
GitHub Issues

Track defined problems and tasks.

GitHub Discussions

Support broader questions and ideas.

Pull Requests

Review concrete repository changes.
```

Avoid scattering decisions across channels without traceability.

---

## Channel Responsibilities

A communication channel should define:

- intended use;
- expected response;
- moderation responsibility;
- decision authority;
- retention;
- confidentiality expectations.

A conversation that creates a significant project decision should be recorded in the appropriate repository artifact.

---

## Communication Principles

Community communication should be:

- respectful;
- relevant;
- evidence-aware;
- understandable;
- inclusive;
- constructive;
- proportionate;
- safe.

Participants should avoid:

- personal attacks;
- public humiliation;
- hostile gatekeeping;
- discriminatory language;
- exposure of confidential information;
- repeated disruption;
- misleading claims of authority;
- pressure for immediate unpaid response.

Disagreement is acceptable.

Abusive behavior is not.

---

## Questions and Learning

The Community should support questions from learners and contributors.

A good response should:

- clarify the problem;
- explain context;
- avoid unnecessary jargon;
- identify assumptions;
- provide safe examples;
- reference documentation;
- encourage verification;
- distinguish fact from opinion.

Questions should not be dismissed only because they appear basic.

Repeated questions may reveal:

- missing documentation;
- unclear navigation;
- weak terminology;
- absent prerequisites;
- incomplete onboarding.

---

## Feedback

Community feedback may address:

- content clarity;
- technical accuracy;
- navigation;
- accessibility;
- contributor experience;
- learning progression;
- missing topics;
- project priorities.

Feedback should identify:

- observed problem;
- context;
- impact;
- evidence;
- proposed improvement when available.

The project may accept, defer, reject or research feedback.

A response should remain respectful and traceable when the feedback affects project scope or Governance.

## Contribution Onboarding

The Contributor Guide remains the primary repository contribution reference.

## First Contributions

First contributions should be approachable and meaningful.

- correcting broken links;

Lower complexity should not mean lower respect for quality.

## Moderation

Moderation protects participants and project continuity.

Moderation may address:

- harassment;
- discriminatory behavior;
- threats;
- spam;
- impersonation;
- confidential-data exposure;
- repeated disruption;
- malicious content;
- unsafe technical instructions;
- abuse of project channels.

Moderation decisions should be:

- proportionate;
- documented when significant;
- confidential when necessary;
- aligned with the Code of Conduct;
- reviewable according to future Governance.

## Reporting Concerns

A future reporting process should explain:

- where to report;
- what information to include;
- who receives the report;
- how confidentiality is handled;
- expected response boundaries;
- urgent safety limitations;
- possible outcomes.

The project should not publish a reporting channel that nobody is responsible for maintaining.

---

## Privacy and Personal Data

Community participation may involve personal information.

The project should minimize collection of:

- full legal names;
- private addresses;
- personal phone numbers;
- identity documents;
- health information;
- private employment information;
- unnecessary demographic data.

Public participation should not require disclosure beyond what is operationally necessary.

---

## Confidential Information

Participants must not publish:

- customer credentials;
- private keys;
- access tokens;
- internal company documents;
- private IP addresses when sensitive;
- personal data;
- production secrets;
- proprietary source code without permission;
- confidential incidents.

Real cases must be anonymized before public discussion or contribution.

---

## Real Cases

Real professional cases may support valuable learning.

Before sharing a case:

- remove names;
- remove credentials;
- remove customer identifiers;
- generalize infrastructure details when necessary;
- separate evidence from assumptions;
- confirm publication rights;
- preserve only the required technical value.

The Community should not encourage exposure as proof of experience.

---

## Technical Safety

Community members may share commands, scripts and procedures.

Technical guidance should identify, when applicable:

- environment;
- required privileges;
- risk;
- backup;
- expected result;
- rollback;
- validation;
- known limitations.

Unsafe instructions should not be presented without context.

Participants should avoid encouraging execution in production environments without appropriate review.

---

## Educational Safety

The Community should avoid:

- shaming learners;
- pretending certainty;
- presenting untested procedures as validated;
- encouraging plagiarism;
- creating false credentials;
- promising guaranteed employment;
- pressuring learners to share personal data.

Learning support should remain honest, evidence-aware and respectful.

---

## Community Decisions

Community discussions may identify options and preferences.

Significant decisions should move through the appropriate mechanism:

- GitHub Issue;
- Decision Log;
- ADR;
- future RFC;
- release planning;
- Governance review.

The final decision should be discoverable outside an informal chat thread.

## Conflict Resolution

Conflict may arise from:

- technical disagreement;
- editorial disagreement;
- unclear responsibility;
- communication style;
- scope pressure;
- review rejection;
- moderation.

Resolution should prioritize:

1. clarification;
2. evidence;
3. applicable Standards;
4. documented responsibilities;
5. respectful mediation;
6. Governance decision when necessary.

Personal conflict should not be transformed into hidden architectural or editorial decisions.

## Community Status

Community initiatives may use statuses such as:

### Idea

The initiative has been identified.

### Research

The need and alternatives are being evaluated.

### Planned

The initiative has been accepted for future implementation.

### Pilot

The initiative is being tested with limited scope.

### Active

The initiative is maintained and available.

### Paused

The initiative temporarily cannot be maintained.

### Retired

The initiative should no longer guide participation.

Status should remain visible when participants depend on the initiative.

---

## Creating a Community Initiative

Before creating a new Community initiative, confirm that:

- a real participation need exists;
- the intended audience is known;
- ownership is defined;
- moderation or support capacity exists;
- the project can maintain the initiative;
- risks are understood;
- privacy is protected;
- success can be evaluated;
- the initiative aligns with current project maturity.

A community idea may remain valuable even when it must be deferred.

---

## Community Review

Community structures should be reviewed for:

### Participant Value

- Does the initiative solve a real need?
- Is the participation path understandable?
- Are expectations realistic?

### Safety

- Are reporting and moderation responsibilities clear?
- Is personal information protected?
- Are risks visible?

### Governance

- Is authority defined?
- Are decisions traceable?
- Does the initiative conflict with active policies?

### Sustainability

- Can maintainers support it?
- Does it create unmanaged expectations?
- Is the workload proportionate?

### Accessibility

- Can participants access the information and activity?
- Are asynchronous options available when practical?

## Success Measures

Community success should not be measured only by participant count.

Possible indicators include:

- useful contributions;
- learner support;
- respectful discussion;
- issue resolution;
- contributor retention;
- documentation improvement;
- review participation;
- safe incident handling;
- reduced onboarding confusion;
- sustainable maintainer workload.

Growth without quality or safety is not sufficient.
