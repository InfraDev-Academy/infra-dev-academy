# ADR-0003 — Editorial Navigation System

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** 2026-07-18

**Version:** 1.0

**Category:** Editorial

**Authors:** InfraDev Academy

---

# Context

The InfraDev Academy considers documentation one of its primary products.

As the number of institutional publications, technical notes, guides and learning packages increases, maintaining consistency across documents becomes essential.

Without editorial standards, publications would gradually lose coherence, making navigation and long-term maintenance more difficult.

The project therefore requires an editorial architecture capable of supporting continuous growth while preserving readability, discoverability and institutional identity.

---

# Decision

All Official Publications shall follow a standardized editorial structure.

Each publication must provide sufficient context for readers to understand:

- what the document is;
- why it exists;
- where it belongs within the Academy;
- how it evolves over time.

The Editorial Navigation System establishes the foundation for a scalable and recognizable publication model.

---

# Editorial Principles

The editorial architecture shall prioritize:

- Consistency
- Readability
- Traceability
- Maintainability
- Discoverability
- Long-term scalability

Documentation is treated as an engineering artifact and therefore follows defined standards.

---

# Standard Publication Structure

Official Publications shall follow this baseline structure:

1. Cover
2. Metadata
3. Preface
4. Main Content
5. Edition Notes
6. Copyright

This structure defines the minimum editorial standard for institutional publications.

---

# Future Evolution

The Editorial Navigation System is designed to evolve.

Future editions may incorporate additional sections such as:

- Relationship Map
- Reading Time
- Audience
- Prerequisites
- Institutional Roadmap

These features are intentionally postponed until they provide measurable value to readers.

---

# Living Publications

Official Publications are considered Living Publications.

Instead of replacing previous documents, each publication evolves through successive editions.

Example:

- First Edition
- Second Edition
- Third Edition

Edition Notes preserve the publication history, making its evolution transparent over time.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Consistent reading experience.
- Strong institutional identity.
- Easier maintenance.
- Improved scalability.
- Better onboarding for readers and contributors.

## Negative

- Slightly longer publication workflow.
- Editorial standards must be maintained consistently.

These costs are justified by the long-term benefits.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Free-form Documentation

Rejected.

Reason:

Produces inconsistent publications and weakens the Academy's identity.

---

## Automatically Generated Documentation

Rejected.

Reason:

Automation cannot replace intentional editorial design and institutional communication.
